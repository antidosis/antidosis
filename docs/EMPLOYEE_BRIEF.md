# Antidosis — Employee Brief

**What we are building, why it matters, and the role you play**

---

## What is Antidosis?

Antidosis is a local needs-exchange platform — a marketplace where neighbours help neighbours. Someone posts a need ("help moving a couch", "fix a leaking tap", "tutor my daughter in maths") and offers something in exchange: money, an item, or another service. The platform matches them with someone nearby who can help, formalises the agreement as a contract, and builds reputation through reviews.

The name comes from the ancient Greek _antidosis_ — the exchange of resources between citizens, where what you give and what you receive balance into something greater for both. That is the idea in one word.

**Current phase:** a pilot on the NSW Central Coast (~350,000 people), with regional expansion planned only after the pilot proves the exchange loop works.

## Why the community needs it

**Everyone is asset-rich in one currency and poor in another.** A retired carpenter has skills and free time but might not want to spend cash. A student has energy and a ute but no income. A young family has a spare room full of things they never use. Antidosis lets people convert what they _have_ — skills, time, stuff — into what they _need_, without everything being a cash transaction.

The practical benefits for a community:

- **Economic resilience.** When money is tight (job loss, rising cost of living, fixed retirement income), the ability to trade skills and goods locally is a real safety net. Barter is legal, and neighbours trading with neighbours keeps value circulating in the community instead of leaking out of it.
- **Skills stay alive and valued.** Decades of experience shouldn't retire when a person does. A platform where a retired electrician's knowledge is _wanted_ — and exchangeable — keeps people engaged, useful, and connected.
- **Less waste, more use.** Items offered in exchange find new owners instead of landfill. Spare capacity (a free Saturday, an empty trailer) gets used instead of sitting idle.
- **Access for the cash-poor, time-rich.** The people with the most time to help are often those with the least money. Antidosis is one of the few marketplaces where that's an advantage, not a barrier.

## How it builds connection

A marketplace of strangers only works if trust is real, so trust is engineered into the product — not bolted on:

- **Verification first.** Identity and mobile verification are required before anyone can post, accept, or message. Verified licence credentials gate regulated work (electrical, plumbing, building) so people hiring help know it's legitimate.
- **Contracts, not handshakes.** Every exchange can be formalised as a written contract with clear terms, in-app messaging, and completion records. Both parties know exactly what was agreed.
- **Reputation that follows you.** Reviews after completed exchanges build a public track record. On Antidosis, your history _is_ your currency.
- **Relay, our community radio.** Named for mesh radio networks, Relay is the in-app comms surface — local channels for the whole neighbourhood, plus Direct messages for one-to-one coordination. It's where the community talks before, during, and after exchanges.
- **Meet your neighbours safely.** The platform is built around meeting in public places for first exchanges, and it gives users the tools (credentials, contracts, reviews, reporting) to make in-person trades feel as safe as they should.

Every exchange is two stories: someone got help they needed, and someone was valued for what they could offer. Do that a few thousand times in one region and you haven't just moved furniture — you've rebuilt the connective tissue of a community.

## Improving our way of life

The bigger picture: modern life has made us extraordinarily connected digitally and oddly disconnected locally. We know what a stranger on another continent had for breakfast but not the name of the person two doors down who could fix our fence. Antidosis is a small, practical counterweight — it gives people a _reason_ to knock, a _structure_ to trust, and a _record_ that says: this neighbourhood takes care of its own.

The goal is not to replace the cash economy or become the next gig giant. It's to prove that a region with a working exchange loop is a better place to live — and then take that proof, one region at a time, further.

## What this means for you as an employee

- **You are an ambassador first.** The pilot lives or dies on liquidity: enough real needs and enough real helpers in one region. Every user you onboard, every need you help fulfil, makes the marketplace more alive for everyone else.
- **Trust is the product.** If you see something that undermines safety, verification, or fairness, it is a top-priority report — not a minor bug. Our users are meeting strangers in person because _we_ told them it was safe.
- **We are careful by design.** No user-to-user funds are held by the platform, contracts are formed directly between users, and regulated work is licence-gated. Keep it that way — the legally safe shape of this product is a feature.
- **Mobile matters most.** For a neighbourhood app, the phone is the primary surface. The person standing in their driveway checking Relay is who we build for.
